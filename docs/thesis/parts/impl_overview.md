This chapter describes the design and implementation of the system developed to address the research objectives. The implementation proceeds in four sequential stages. The first stage covers data ingestion and pre-processing: a three-million-row CSV export from Tallinn's IoT acoustic monitoring network is parsed, timestamps are normalised to Tallinn local time and truncated to hourly boundaries, sub-minute readings are aggregated into hourly medians using the median to suppress transient noise spikes, and missing hours are quantified across all 471 devices — revealing a fleet-wide gap rate of approximately 26%. The second stage implements four independent imputation methods of increasing complexity: a historical median baseline that exploits the strong daily periodicity of urban noise, a spatial K-Nearest Neighbours method that draws on concurrent readings from geographically proximate sensors, an inverse-variance weighted combination of the two that adapts dynamically to whichever source is more reliable for each individual slot, and Google TimesFM 2.5, a pre-trained time series foundation model applied in zero-shot mode to re-impute slots where sufficient temporal context is available. The third stage validates the imputation methods through a stratified masked evaluation: 20% of original readings are withheld from 35 carefully selected test devices, grouped into three categories — spatially connected, spatially isolated, and short-history — designed to expose the distinct failure modes of each method, and accuracy is measured using MAE and RMSE in decibels against the held-out ground truth. The fourth stage presents the results through an interactive dashboard built with React, MapLibre GL, and Recharts, enabling spatial and temporal exploration of all four imputation tables alongside a quantitative comparison of method accuracy.
